TriageAI — Thai Seller Review Dashboard

Tells a seller what's actually going wrong, from their Thai customer reviews.

Code on GitHub

A web app that reads a seller’s Thai-language customer reviews and tells them what’s actually going wrong. Upload a CSV and it classifies every review as positive, neutral, or negative, charts the breakdown, then sends the negative ones to an LLM to summarize the recurring complaints and suggest fixes — in Thai.

The classifier is a TF-IDF + Logistic Regression model trained on the ~27,000-sample Wisesight Thai sentiment dataset, reaching about 70% accuracy across three classes after a Thai-specific preprocessing pipeline (normalization, tokenization, slang handling). Negative reviews get passed to Gemini 2.5 Flash for theme extraction and recommendations.

Built with

PyThaiNLPscikit-learnTF-IDF Gemini 2.5 FlashStreamlit

How it works

  1. Clean the text: normalise Thai characters, strip links, hashtags, mentions and emoji, shorten stretched words like ดีมากกกกก, and turn <3 into รัก.
  2. Split it into words with PyThaiNLP, since Thai is written without spaces.
  3. Turn each review into TF-IDF features, from single words and word pairs.
  4. Classify it as positive, neutral or negative with logistic regression.
  5. Send up to 30 negative reviews to Gemini 2.5 Flash twice: once to group the complaints into three to five themes, once for an overall summary with advice for the seller, both in Thai.
  6. Download it all as a text report.

Choosing the classifier

We compared four classic text classifiers on 2,617 held-out messages: logistic regression, a linear SVM, and two kinds of naive Bayes. Logistic regression had the best accuracy, by a narrow margin, so it's the one in the app.

An example run

On the included sample of 223 reviews, rated 3.0 out of 5 with about half of them negative, Gemini grouped the complaints into products not as described, slow delivery, damage in shipping, missing items, and a shop that's slow to reply.

Limits

TF-IDF counts words without understanding them, so ของดี (good) and ของไม่ดี (not good) look almost the same to it. Positive reviews are the hardest class because the training data has the fewest of them. A fine-tuned Thai language model such as WangchanBERTa would be the next step.
