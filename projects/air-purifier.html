Smart Air Purifier with PM2.5 Monitoring

Measures the air and turns the fan on by itself when it gets worse, or when someone walks in.

An ESP32-based smart-home device that continuously measures airborne particulates (PM1.0 / PM2.5 / PM10) with a laser sensor and automatically switches a fan on when air quality drops or a person is detected. Built on a three-layer IoT architecture with live readings on an on-device OLED and streamed to the Blynk cloud for remote monitoring.

Covers the hardware wiring, the Arduino (C++) firmware, and the project report.

Built with

ESP32Arduino / C++PMS5003PIR sensorBlynk

How it decides

The fan doesn't use one fixed threshold. When someone is in the room it switches on early; when the room is empty it waits, which saves power and filter life.

Someone in the roomfan on above 25 µg/m³
Room emptyfan on above 55 µg/m³

A motion sensor misses people who sit still, so the device counts the room as occupied for 15 seconds after the last movement it sees.

How it's built

  • Three layers: the sensors (a PMS5003 laser particle sensor and a PIR motion sensor), the ESP32, and two displays, the OLED on the device and the Blynk app on a phone.
  • Every decision is made on the ESP32, so the purifier keeps working when the Wi-Fi drops. Blynk only gets a copy of the readings every 5 seconds.
  • The firmware finds the start of each 32-byte frame from the PMS5003 and checks its checksum before trusting a reading.
  • The 12 V fan has its own power supply, switched through a relay, so it can't disturb the ESP32 and the sensors.

Testing it

With smoke held near the sensor in a small box, PM2.5 went from 6 µg/m³ to 587 within seconds, and 30 seconds after the fan started it was back down to 19. All four cases, occupied or empty with clean or dirty air, switched the fan as designed.

The box wasn't sealed, so not all of that drop is the filter. The test shows that the sensor and the fan logic respond correctly, not how well the purifier actually cleans a room.

What went wrong

  • The PMS5003 uses a 1.25 mm 8-pin connector, and we couldn't find a ready-made cable for it, so we soldered a bare-wire cable onto jumper leads ourselves.
  • A 0.96-inch screen can't fit everything, so it alternates between a live-status page and an air-quality scale every 5 seconds.
  • The sensor needs about 30 seconds to settle after power-on, so the boot screen says "Warming PMS5003…" while it does.
  • Next steps would be a HEPA filter, a sealed enclosure, a stronger fan, and logging the history instead of only showing the live value.
