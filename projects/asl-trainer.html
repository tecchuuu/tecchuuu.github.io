ASL Fingerspelling Trainer

Practise the ASL alphabet with your webcam and get feedback on every letter.

Code on GitHub

What it does

A web app for practising American Sign Language fingerspelling. You sign a letter to your webcam, MediaPipe finds the 21 points of your hand in the browser, and a small MLP names the letter. It's a practice tool, not a translator: it takes over alphabet drill so a teacher's time can go to teaching.

  • Learn: every letter with a reference photo, a handshape tip and the letters it's easily mixed up with
  • Practice by category, spelling whole words letter by letter, with hints and retries
  • Exam: no reference photo, scored on letters right the first try
  • Free spell: sign anything; a pause adds a space
  • My progress and a trainer dashboard showing where a class gets stuck

What I found

I compared the landmark MLP with a CNN trained on hand photos. On the dataset they scored almost the same. On a real webcam the CNN fell apart: it had learned the look of the dataset's one room and one signer. The MLP only sees the shape of the hand, so it kept working in a new room, with the other hand, and in the dark.

Limits

It covers the 24 static letters. J and Z need motion, which would take a sequence model, and so would fast continuous spelling. The webcam test used one signer.

My role

A solo project: the data pipeline, training and tuning both models, the webcam evaluation, and the web app.
